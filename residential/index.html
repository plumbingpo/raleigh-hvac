Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Nobody likes surprises on the final bill. So Raleigh HVAC quotes every Raleigh job upfront, after a full diagnostic that looks at the equipment's age, fuel type, and tonnage, refrigerant levels, coil and heat exchanger condition, and the ductwork feeding each room. You'll know what's wrong, what the fix involves, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

The problem with HVAC failures is that most of them develop out of sight. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil slowly drains refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down a little at a time, and a weak capacitor holds on until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. When bills climb, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most air conditioners and furnaces last 12 to 18 years, and a planned replacement is nearly always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap during a heat wave or cold snap. Our technicians know the climate demands common to Raleigh and how they affect equipment life, so we can tell you honestly whether your system has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Maintenance Plans for Raleigh Homes

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the whole point of our maintenance plans. On each scheduled visit, we clean coils and burners, check the refrigerant charge, test the electrical components, inspect the heat exchanger, flush the condensate drain, and change the filter, all matched to the equipment in your home.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with spring cooling tune-ups and fall heating tune-ups for most systems. That's especially important in dusty areas, where dirty condenser coils choke heat rejection and drive up energy use. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your equipment's age, your household's comfort needs, and your local climate.

Consider maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already bought. Each tune-up keeps the coils clean, the charge correct, and the filter fresh, and that lowers monthly energy bills while easing the strain on the compressor and blower. For most families, a plan costs far less than replacing the system ahead of schedule.

Sized for Your Home

The Right Size System for Your Home

Proper sizing is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before quoting new equipment, Raleigh HVAC measures the details that drive your home's heating and cooling load: square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout. That gives you a system that runs in long, efficient cycles, keeps humidity in check, and keeps every room at the temperature you set.

Homes change over time. An addition or a finished basement can overwhelm the single-stage system that used to be plenty. Today's two-stage and variable-speed equipment holds tighter temperatures and uses far less energy. Whether you're comparing a heat pump and a furnace-and-AC pair, or ducted and ductless, we'll explain the honest pros and cons in SEER2 ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting needs, and installation work.

Repairs We Handle

Heating and Cooling Problems We Solve in Raleigh

Warm air from the vents, a furnace that refuses to light, ice on the indoor coil, a slow refrigerant leak: every one of these problems has a different cause and needs a different repair. Raleigh HVAC diagnoses the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's a condenser that hums but won't start, a short-cycling furnace, a thermostat that loses its settings, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split throwing error codes.

Not every HVAC problem is equally urgent. A weak capacitor just keeps the compressor from starting. A cracked heat exchanger, on the other hand, can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a backed-up condensate drain can soak through a ceiling before anyone notices. We come prepared to replace capacitors, contactors, fan motors, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards, repair leaky ducts that dump conditioned air into the attic, and recharge refrigerant once a leak has been repaired.
